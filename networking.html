What is networking?

When people hear the word networking, they often think about business events, conferences and awkward conversations with strangers but in reality, networking is simply about building relationships with other people. It is about meeting people who share similar interests, learning from them and creating connections that may help you in the future and in the games industry, networking can happen almost anywhere. It could be through university classes, online communities, game jams, Discord servers or professional platforms such as LinkedIn. The games industry relies heavily on teamwork, so being able to communicate with people and build professional relationships is an important skill.

Discord logo © Discord Inc.

Why networking matters in the games industry

The games industry is built around collaboration with large studios such as Bethesda are made up of programmers, artists, designers, writers, sound engineers and testers who all work together to create a finished product. Without communication and teamwork, projects on the scale of Fallout would not be possible but also networking is important because it creates opportunities by speaking with developers, joining communities and connecting with professionals can help people find jobs, gain experience and learn new skills. Employers often look for people who can work well with others, communicate effectively and contribute positively to a team environment and as a Games Development student, networking is something I need to take seriously. Technical skills are important, but they are not the only thing employers are looking for but also being able to introduce yourself, discuss your work and build professional relationships is equally valuable.

The Fallout community as a networking example

One of the reasons I chose Fallout as the main theme of this website is because of the size and dedication of its community, with even years after release, people are still creating mods, sharing theories, discussing lore and helping new players with communities around Fallout exist on Reddit, Discord, YouTube, Nexus Mods and many other platforms. Fallout New Vegas is a particularly good example since despite being released in 2010, it still has an active modding scene with players creating new quests, locations, gameplay features with these projects often involve multiple people working together and sharing ideas and, in many ways this is networking in action. Projects such as Fallout London show what can happen when passionate developers connect through a shared interest and decide to work together and while many of the people involved may have started as fans, networking allowed them to collaborate on a project that gained attention across the gaming community.

Fallout London promotional artwork © Team FOLON / Bethesda Softworks.

Networking online vs networking in person

Today, networking can be done both online and in person, with online networking is probably the easiest place to start because it allows people to connect regardless of where they live with such platforms like LinkedIn, Discord and GitHub make it possible to share projects, communicate with developers and learn from industry professionals. Online networking also allows people to build connections gradually by commenting on projects, asking questions and sharing your own work can help others become familiar with who you are and what you do. In person networking still has advantages though with industry events, conferences, university workshops and game jams allow people to meet face to face and have conversations that are often more memorable with events such as EGX and Develop Brighton provide opportunities for students to learn from professionals and make valuable contacts. I think the best approach is a combination of both, because online networking allows you to stay connected throughout the year, while in person events can help strengthen those relationships.

Researching the industry

Before trying to network with people, I think it is important to understand the industry you are entering and by this, I mean the ways of researching companies, developers and current trends and in my own field, this includes studios such as Rivet Games, Dovetail, 4J Studios and Blazing Griffin. Understanding what companies do and what types of games they create makes networking more meaningful because you already have some knowledge of the people and organisations you are trying to connect with and it also helps when applying for jobs because you can demonstrate a genuine interest in the industry rather than approaching companies without understanding their work.

What can I learn from this?

One lesson I have learned is that networking does not happen overnight as building relationships takes time and requires effort because it is something that needs to be developed gradually through regular interaction and communication and I have also learned that networking is not just about finding job opportunities but its about learning from others, sharing experiences and becoming part of a wider community and this is something that the Fallout community demonstrates very well.

The platforms I would use

If I was building my professional network today, there are a few platforms I would focus on; LinkedIn would be one of the most important because it allows students and professionals to connect directly and it can also be used to showcase projects, share achievements and follow companies that you may want to work for in the future. GitHub is another platform that I think is essential for Games Development students as it allows developers to demonstrate their coding skills and provides employers with examples of their work. I also think Discord and Reddit can be useful and while they are less formal, they allow developers to join communities, participate in discussions and receive feedback on projects and these platforms can often be good places to meet people who share similar interests.

Reddit logo © Reddit Inc.

My conclusion

Networking is an important part of building a career in the games industry while, technical skills are essential, the relationships we build with other people can often be just as important. The Fallout community provides a great example of how people can come together through a shared interest, collaborate on projects and support one another and as a Games Development student, networking is something I need to continue developing throughout my studies. Whether it be through LinkedIn, GitHub, online communities or industry events, building connections will help me learn new skills, discover opportunities and prepare for a future career in game development.
