What are IP rights?

When people hear the term Intellectual Property, or IP, it can sound like a complicated legal concept. In reality, it's simply a way of protecting creative work and for game developers, this includes things such as artwork, characters, stories, music, code and even well known brand names. Without IP rights, it would be much easier for people to copy someone else's work and claim it as their own and for a studio such as Bethesda, protecting its intellectual property is important because franchises such as Fallout have taken years to build.

Why does IP matter?

As a Games Development student, I think IP rights are important because they help protect the time and effort that goes into creating a game since developing a successful game can take years of work and involve hundreds of people. When I think about Fallout, I don't just think about the games themselves, I also think about iconic parts of the series such as the Pip-Boy, power armour, Vault Boy and the overall retro futuristic style that makes Fallout instantly recognisable and these elements help make Fallout unique, and IP law exists to help protect them.

Fallout and community creativity

One thing I have always liked about Fallout is its modding community. Games such as Fallout New Vegas and Fallout 4 have thousands of mods that improve graphics, add quests and create entirely new experiences and in my opinion, mods show how creativity can build upon an existing game without necessarily replacing it. Many modders create content because they enjoy the games and want to contribute to the community but, there is still a line between creating fan content and using someone else's intellectual property in a way that infringes on ownership rights.

AI enters the picture

This is where things start becoming more complicated since over the last few years, AI tools have become increasingly common within creative industries. AI can generate images, write stories, create code and even produce voices that sound realistic and while supporters of AI often argue that it improves productivity and allows developers to work faster. I can somewhat understand that argument but, I also think there are serious concerns about how these tools are trained and how they use existing creative work and many AI systems learn by analysing huge amounts of content created by artists, writers and developers. This raises an important question which is, if an AI learns from someone else's work, who actually owns the final result?

The Fallout AI controversy

One example that caught my attention recently involved an AI generated browser game called Fallout: New York. The project used AI to create a Fallout inspired experience and quickly gained attention online but, it was later removed after Bethesda's parent company, ZeniMax, reportedly issued a cease and desist notice related to intellectual property concerns and for me, this situation highlights one of the biggest problems with AI generated content. While the technology itself might be impressive, it becomes difficult to ignore the fact that it relies heavily on intellectual property that belongs to someone else and with Fallout which was created through decades of work by hundreds of developers, artists and designers and simply using AI to recreate that work does not mean ownership suddenly disappears, it means you're using the work of others.

AI and legal disputes

The Fallout: New York situation is not the only example of AI raising questions about intellectual property since there have been several legal disputes in recent years involving AI generated content and one of the most notable cases involved a lawsuit against Stability AI, the company behind the popular image generation tool, Stable Diffusion. The lawsuit alleged that Stability AI used copyrighted images to train its system without permission and while the case is still ongoing, it demonstrates how quickly questions about ownership and copyright can emerge when technology interacts with established intellectual property.

Who benefits and who loses?

One reason AI remains controversial is because different groups are affected differently with the people who benefit are often those who want content produced quickly and cheaply. AI can generate artwork, scripts and other assets within seconds but, the people who may lose out are often the creators whose work helped train those systems in the first place and artists, writers, musicians and designers can spend years developing their skills, only to find AI systems producing similar content almost instantly. As someone studying Games Development, I find this concerning and while AI can be a useful tool in other ways maybe, I don't believe it should replace the people responsible for creating original work.

My opinion on AI

Personally, I don't see AI as a tool nor a creator since if a developer uses AI to help brainstorm ideas or automate repetitive tasks, that may seem reasonable to other, however I become much more sceptical when AI is used to generate artwork, writing or entire projects based heavily on work created by other people. Part of what makes games special is the creativity behind them and with Fallout it's memorable because of the developers, artists and writers who created it, not because an algorithm generated content based on existing material.

My conclusion

IP rights play an important role in protecting creative work and ensuring that creators receive recognition for their effort and the Fallout franchise demonstrates why this protection matters, as its identity has been built over decades through the work of talented developers and artists. While AI may be a potential to become a useful tool within game development, I believe it also creates significant challenges since the recent AI generated Fallout project demonstrates how quickly questions about ownership and copyright can emerge when technology interacts with established intellectual property. As a future game developer, I believe creativity should continue to be valued and protected and maybe technology may change how games are made, but the people behind those games should not be forgotten.
