What does professionalism really mean?

When most people hear the word professionalism, they usually think about wearing a suit, showing up on time and acting professionally in the workplace. While those things are important, I think professionalism in the games industry goes much further than that. For game studios, professionalism is about how they manage projects, communicate with players, respond to criticism and support their games after release. It is also about building trust with a community and delivering the best experience possible for players. When thinking about this topic, Bethesda Game Studios immediately came to mind. As one of the biggest names in the role playing game industry, Bethesda has experienced both huge successes and major criticism over the years, making it a useful example of what professionalism can look like in practice.

Building a reputation

Bethesda has spent decades building its reputation through franchises such as The Elder Scrolls and Fallout. Games like Oblivion, Skyrim, Fallout 3 and Fallout 4 helped establish the studio as one of the biggest developers in gaming. As a Fallout fan, part of the reason I keep coming back to the series is because Bethesda creates worlds that feel unique. Exploring abandoned vaults, travelling across the Capital Wasteland and wandering around the Commonwealth created experiences that players still talk about years later. Creating great games is one thing, but maintaining a strong reputation for years requires consistency. Players need to trust that a studio can deliver quality experiences.

Professionalism is not about being perfect

One thing I realised while researching this topic is that professionalism does not mean being perfect. If that was true, Bethesda would probably not be the best example. Many Bethesda games have launched with bugs and technical issues, and jokes about Bethesda bugs have almost become part of gaming culture. While players often laugh about them, technical issues can damage a studio's reputation if they are not handled properly. The biggest example of this is Fallout 76. When it launched in 2018, the reaction from many players was overwhelmingly negative. The game suffered from bugs, technical problems and missing features. To be honest, I was one of those disappointed players. After spending years enjoying Fallout 3, New Vegas and Fallout 4, Fallout 76 felt like a huge step backwards. Looking back, however, I think Fallout 76 actually provides one of the strongest examples of professionalism in gaming. Bethesda could have moved on and focused on other projects, but instead they continued supporting the game.

New updates were released, NPCs were added, story content was expanded and many issues were fixed. Fallout 76 is still not my favourite game in the series, but I respect the fact that Bethesda continued improving it instead of abandoning it. To me, professionalism is not about avoiding mistakes. It is about taking responsibility for them and working to improve.

The Four Pillars of Professionalism

One idea from the lectures that stood out to me was Kizza's Four Pillars of Professionalism: Commitment, Integrity, Responsibility and Accountability. Looking at Bethesda's handling of Fallout 76, I think all four can be seen in different ways. While the launch received significant criticism, Bethesda continued supporting the game instead of abandoning it. They released updates, listened to feedback and worked to improve the overall experience. Whether players agree with every decision or not, I think this demonstrates Commitment and Responsibility. It also highlights Accountability because the studio continued working on the product after launch rather than pretending the problems did not exist.

Building and maintaining communities

Another important part of professionalism is how a company interacts with its community. The Fallout community remains active today despite some of the games being over a decade old. Fallout New Vegas still has a thriving modding scene, while Fallout 4 continues to receive fan made content. Bethesda has generally encouraged modding and community involvement, helping players stay engaged long after release. This relationship between developers and players is important because communities often determine how successful a game remains over time. A professional studio understands that players are not simply customers. They are also a key part of a game's long term success.

Professionalism in a team environment

Another aspect of professionalism that is often overlooked is the ability to work effectively with other people. Modern games are rarely created by a single developer. Large projects such as Fallout involve programmers, designers, artists, writers, sound engineers and testers all working together towards the same goal. Without good communication and teamwork, projects can quickly run into problems. Even as a Games Development student, I have already seen how important these skills can be. When working on projects, team members need to communicate clearly, meet deadlines and be willing to support one another when issues arise. Technical ability is important, but employers also want people who can work well with others and contribute positively to a team. Bethesda's games may not always launch perfectly, but creating worlds as large and detailed as those found in the Fallout series would not be possible without collaboration between hundreds of people. Professionalism is therefore not only about individual behaviour. It is also about respecting colleagues, communicating effectively and contributing to a successful team environment.

Professionalism in a team environment

Another part of professionalism that I think sometimes gets overlooked is teamwork. Modern games are rarely made by one person and large projects such as Fallout require programmers, designers, artists, writers, sound engineers and testers to work together. Without good communication and teamwork, even the best ideas can run into problems. As a Games Development student, I've already started to see how important this can be. When working on projects, everyone needs to communicate clearly, meet deadlines and be willing to help when problems come up. Being good at programming or design is important, but employers also want people who can work well with others and contribute positively to a team. When you look at games such as Fallout 3, Fallout 4 or even Fallout 76, it's easy to focus on the final product, but behind the scenes there are hundreds of people working together to make those games happen. Bethesda's games may not always launch perfectly, but creating worlds on that scale would not be possible without strong teamwork and collaboration. To me, professionalism is not just about how you behave as an individual, but also about how you support the people around you and contribute to a successful team.

My conclusion

Bethesda is a good example of how professionalism in the creative industries is about much more than simply creating successful products. It involves building trust, maintaining relationships with communities and responding positively when challenges arise. While Fallout 3, Fallout New Vegas and Fallout 4 helped make Bethesda one of the most recognisable studios in gaming, I think Fallout 76 provides the most valuable lesson. Its difficult launch and gradual recovery show that professionalism is not about being perfect. It is about how a company responds when things do not go according to plan. As someone hoping to work in the games industry, I think that lesson is just as important as any technical skill I learn during my time at UWS.
